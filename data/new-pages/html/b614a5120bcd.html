Tone Tags for AI speakers

You can now add inline tone directions to your AI speech scripts when using ElevenLabs V3 speakers. A Tone Tag is a small chip that appears directly in your transcript at the insertion point — it tells ElevenLabs v3 how to deliver the surrounding speech. Think whispers, laughs, pauses, excitement, or anything you want to describe in plain language.

To add one, place your cursor in the transcript and click Add tone in the toolbar. A preset picker appears with common options (happy, sad, whispers, laughs, pause, curious, and more). You can also type a custom direction — ElevenLabs treats it as a prompting instruction, so shorter and more specific tends to work better than long descriptions.

When you add or edit a tone tag, the surrounding paragraph regenerates automatically with the new tone applied. Paragraph breaks are the natural way to reset tone — the model determines how far the effect carries from each insertion point, so what you're really controlling is placement.

Tone Tags are available for ElevenLabs v3 speakers only. On any other speaker, inline notes remain regular in-line notes.

How to find it

  1. Assign an ElevenLabs v3 speaker to a clip
  2. Click in the transcript where you want to add a tone direction
  3. Select Add tone from the toolbar
  4. Choose a preset or enter a custom direction

A new Effects drawer and 10 new effects

We've redesigned the Effects panel and added ten new effects to the lineup. The new Effects drawer lives in the Properties panel. Hover over any effect to preview it on the current frame, then click to apply.

The new effects:

  • Portrait Lighting* — keeps the main subject lit while dimming the background, so they stand out in the foreground
  • Uplighting* — automatically brightens the whole video for low-light footage
  • Ascii — renders the video as a grid of text characters
  • VHS — adds a retro analog-tape look with scan lines, color bleeding, and signal artifacts (CRT TV, VHS, VHS EP, and Tracking error styles)
  • Color tone — converts video to monochrome and tints it with custom colors
  • Selective color — keeps a selected color in the video and converts everything else to black and white
  • Pixelate — applies a blocky, pixelated effect for style or light obfuscation
  • Threshold — converts the image to pure black and white for a high-contrast graphic look
  • Invert — inverts the colors of the layer for a film-negative look
  • Vignette — darkens the edges of the frame to focus attention on the center

*These items use AI processing and therefore use AI credits.

How to find it

  1. In the scene editor, select a scene or layer
  2. Click Effects in the hover toolbar, then + Add effect
  3. Hover any effect in the drawer to preview it, then click to apply

Underlord updates—context, chat history, and improved edit review

Add context

Underlord now lets you pin specific parts of your project directly to a request. Click the @-button in the Underlord chat panel to open the context picker and attach files, scenes, timestamps, or individual layers before you describe what you want done.

This is most useful in complex projects where Underlord might otherwise have trouble knowing what you're referring to. Instead of describing the scene, just attach it.

How to find it

  1. Open a project and open the Underlord panel
  2. Type @ in the chat input
  3. Select files, scenes, timestamps, or layers from the picker
  4. Add your request and send

Chat history

Your Underlord conversations now persist. Refreshing the page, navigating away, or switching projects no longer wipes your chat. When you come back to a project, Underlord picks up where you left off.

Past conversations are accessible from the Underlord panel, listed in reverse chronological order and auto-titled based on their contents. You can browse older chats and reuse prompts that worked well — useful for complex projects where you've built up a lot of context with Underlord over multiple sessions.

A few details on how it works:

  • Per-user, per-project. Chat history is scoped to you and the project you're in. Collaborators on the same project can't see your chats.
  • Older chats are read-only. You can view past conversations but can only continue chatting (and rolling back) from your current open chat.
  • Syncs across browsers and devices, web and desktop.
  • New chat button remains available if you want a fresh start.

How to find it

  1. Open any project and open the Underlord panel
  2. Your previous conversation loads automatically
  3. To browse older chats, look for the chat history list at the top of the panel

Improved edit reviews

We've added an automatic review step after every Underlord editing turn. After Underlord makes a change, a second pass checks the diff against what you actually asked for, looking specifically for things like unintended deletions, edits that missed their target, overcorrections, or operations that went out of scope. If something looks wrong, Underlord is re-invoked automatically to fix it.

You don't interact with this directly—it runs in the background. The practical effect is fewer cases where Underlord does something technically correct but contextually wrong.

New file format support

Descript now imports a significantly wider range of media formats. These were selected based on how frequently they were hitting unsupported file errors, from most to least common:

  • MKV / Matroska — a general-purpose container; Descript now supports all the codecs inside MKV that it already handles in other formats
  • AVIF images — the AV1-based image format, now widely available across major browsers
  • Opus audio (.opus) — OGG container with Opus codec, common in web audio and recordings
  • Raw AAC (.aac) — ADTS container format, reasonably common
  • WebM — with VP8 and VP9 video and Opus audio; the open-source format promoted by Google and used widely on YouTube
  • Multi-channel / surround audio — 5.1 and other surround files are now accepted. By default they're downmixed to stereo. You can also split the channels into individual mono clips inside the app.

Workspaces, Drive sidebar, and Settings redesigns

The main navigation areas of Descript have a new look. Workspaces are cleaner and easier to move around in, the Drive sidebar has been reorganized for faster scanning, and Settings has been restructured so related controls are grouped more logically.

Enterprise drives now have access to Custom Workspaces, so larger teams can organize projects into tailored spaces (e.g., by department or initiative), with clearer navigation and less clutter as your Drive grows.

Improved transcription with ElevenLabs Scribe v2

Scribe v2 is now the default transcription model across all supported languages. It outperformed our other models in testing, resulting in fewer corrections per minute and fewer capitalization fixes.

What changed:

  • Automatic mode now routes to Scribe v2 (previously Rev v2 for English, now Scribe v2 for all languages)
  • The model picker now shows real model names: Automatic, Scribe v2, and Rev v2
  • British English: Scribe v2 uses American English spelling. To get British spelling, select Rev v2 from the model picker

See Automatic transcription and Supported transcription languages for more.

GPT Image 2 is now available as a model option in Descript's Generate media panel. It's notable for character consistency and performs especially well on text-heavy images and infographic-style compositions.

How to find it

  1. Open the AI Tools panel → Generate media
  2. Choose Image, then open the Model dropdown
  3. Select GPT Image 2

Bullet points in Canvas text clips

You can now add bullet point formatting to text clips in the Canvas.

Select a text clip of any kind, and enter a dash [-] and hit Return to format it into a bullet point or bulleted list.

  • Publish triggers — initiate a publish from an external workflow
  • Project search — find projects programmatically
  • Live progress in Claude and ChatGPT — when using the MCP connection, you can see real-time task progress

]]> 6945e963a88aa53ad74ae6ad https://descript.canny.io/changelog/release-roundupdecember-20th-2025 Sat, 20 Dec 2025 15:00:00 GMT Descript Changelog Release roundup—December 20th, 2025 Gabe Michalski new YouTube import has been turned off

We’ve made the decision to turn off the YouTube import feature in Descript for now. We want to share a bit of context about why.

What happened

Over the past several months, YouTube imports have become increasingly unreliable. Success rates dropped significantly, and many videos simply stopped importing altogether.

Why it has been unreliable

YouTube regularly updates its systems to prevent automated downloading. These changes directly affect tools like ours, and they can break or limit importing with little notice. We’ve evaluated short-term and long-term options, but at this point we are not able to provide a version of YouTube import that works consistently over the longterm.

We hope to revisit this in the future, especially if conditions change, but we don’t want to offer a feature that fails unpredictably or creates confusion.

What this means for you

You can still work with YouTube content in Descript you’ll just need to download the videos manually first, then import the files directly into your project. We know this adds an extra step, and we’re sorry for the inconvenience.

How to download videos from YouTube Studio

If you’re downloading your own YouTube videos, the most reliable option is YouTube Studio, where you can download the original files you uploaded.

Here’s how:

  • 1. Open YouTube Studio

Go to YouTube Studio and sign in with the account that owns the video.

  • 2. Go to Your Content

Click Content in the left sidebar to view your uploads.

  • 3. Download the Video

Hover over the video, click the Options menu (three dots), and select Download.

You can only download videos you own; YouTube doesn’t allow downloading other creators’ content from Studio.

  • 4. Import into Descript

Drag the downloaded file into Descript or add it from the Project panel.

Using YouTube Studio is the most stable, YouTube-compliant way to access your own videos and avoids the issues that affected our previous import feature.
